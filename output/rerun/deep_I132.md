## One-line
Cover (about EUR 39 bundle incl. VAT) plus annual dated refill (about EUR 19) with Swedish/Danish layouts and school-term grids; designer cover collabs.

## Moat mechanism (honest)
- Claimed: annual refill lock-in plus locale content (SE/DK week numbering, name days, public holidays, school-term grids) plus designer collabs.
- Legal protection obtainable: an EU registered design for the cover or page layout costs roughly EUR 350-400 filing for one design (ASSUMPTION, check EUIPO fee table), takes weeks, and protects appearance only. It does not stop a rival from printing a functionally identical Swedish layout in a different look.
- Refill format lock-in only works if the cover takes a proprietary size/binding. Choosing a proprietary format reduces the addressable base (nobody owns it yet), and A5/A6 ring or disc systems already exist as standards (Filofax, Hobonichi sizes) that rivals can fit.
- Circumvention: any printer can add SE/DK layouts in weeks; Hobonichi, Papier, Moleskine, Burde/Mayland-type Nordic calendar publishers can localise at near-zero marginal cost. Paper (Tomoe-type 52 gsm) is not exclusive.
- Verdict on moat: habit and community, not legal. Honest defensibility 2.

## Market (bottom-up, all ASSUMPTION, confidence Low)
- Method: adults aged 16-45 x share who buy a premium (EUR 25+) paper planner x annual spend.
- SE+DK population about 16.6M (SE ~10.5M, DK ~6M); 16-45 about 5.2M. At 3% premium planner buyers = about 156k buyers x EUR 30/yr = about EUR 4.7M.
- Nordics (adds NO, FI) about EUR 8M SAM.
- EU TAM: 160M aged 16-45 x 1.5% x EUR 30 = about EUR 70M (range 70-150M).
- SOM year 1 for two part-timers (10 h/week combined): 400-800 customers, about EUR 12-25k net revenue after VAT. Planners are bought in a Nov-Jan window plus an Aug-Sep academic-year window, so the 10 h/week constraint is binding at peak.

## Personas
1. Stationery hobbyist (25-40, Instagram/TikTok "planner with me", already buys Hobonichi/Midori): wants a local-language layout, will pay EUR 40+.
2. Student/teacher/parent running an academic year (Aug-Jul) who wants school term, holiday and vacation weeks in one view.
3. Corporate gifting buyer (small firm HR/office manager) buying 50-200 branded planners; low CAC if reached through founder network, but price-sensitive and low-margin.

## Jobs to be done
- Plan the week on paper with correct local calendar (week numbers, holidays, school terms) without hand-editing an imported planner.
- Keep one object for the whole year that feels designed and personal.
- Replace the refill every year without re-learning a system.

## Channel and CAC
- Paid social to Nordic stationery audiences: assumed CAC EUR 20-35 for a EUR 39 bundle (ASSUMPTION; typical consumer e-commerce benchmark, not sourced).
- Organic: planner-creator content on TikTok/Instagram can be near-zero CAC but is unreliable and time-hungry on 10 h/week.
- Wholesale to bookshops/stationers: retailer margin about 45-50% leaves contribution of EUR 4-6 per bundle.
- B2B gifting through founder networks: low CAC, but low volume and small margin.
- Amazon EU: planners are a crowded category with private label; rated as poor fit.

## Regulatory checklist (only what applies)
- GPSR: responsible person/contact and traceability on product or packaging (non-EU supplier needs an EU economic operator).
- PPWR/packaging: paper packaging labelling and Swedish/Danish producer-responsibility registration for packaging (SE Naturvardsverket-style registers, DK; ASSUMPTION, verify).
- REACH/chemical content for inks, glues and cover materials; request supplier declarations.
- If cover uses animal or synthetic leather claims, no "vegan" or "recycled" claims without evidence (Green Claims/consumer law).
- VAT OSS for cross-border B2C; both SE and DK standard VAT 25% (ASSUMPTION, High confidence from general knowledge).
- Designer collab: written licence for artwork; EU registered design filing if desired.

## Unit economics (ASSUMPTION, per bundle, SE/DK at 25% VAT)
- Price EUR 39 incl. VAT = EUR 31.2 net.
- COGS: refill EUR 4.5, cover EUR 7, pack EUR 0.8 (at about 500 units); parcel delivery EUR 4; payment fees EUR 1.2.
- Contribution before marketing: about EUR 13.7 (44%).
- Refill only (EUR 19 incl. VAT = EUR 15.2 net): COGS 4.5 + 0.5 pack + 3.5 shipping + 0.7 fees, contribution about EUR 6.
- Retention: assume 40% buy a year-2 refill, 20% year 3 -> repeat contribution about EUR 4 expected per customer.
- LTV contribution about EUR 18 vs paid CAC EUR 20-35: **negative after CAC**. Contribution after CAC on the first order is -EUR 6 to -EUR 21.
- Only organic/B2B acquisition (CAC under EUR 8) makes it work; wholesale makes it worse.
- First order: about 500 refills + 300 covers + foil die/samples/design/legal = about EUR 5.5-6.5k, within the soft cap. Tooling is minimal (digital print; cover foil die about EUR 150-300, ASSUMPTION), so capital is not the blocker.
- Dated-stock risk: the 2027 refill is worthless after Dec 2027 (unsold units are a write-off); the Techo-style product is dated by nature. Practical launch window is the 2027/28 academic-year edition (Aug 2027), not 2027 (too late today).

## Differentiation we could own
Swedish and Danish school-term/vacation-week grids in a Techo-style layout plus a collab artist series. Real but narrow, and copyable. A variant with more defensible channel: a site-diary/installer logbook for the building trade distributed through the founders' ex-VELUX installer and housing association contacts (annual consumable refill, B2B channel). This is speculative and untested.

## Verdict
**Park as a consumer product.** The original scores (trend 4, margin 4, EU 5, channel 3) were too generous. It passes capital limits but fails the re-run test on moat and on contribution after CAC. Revisit only if the validation test below shows organic demand, or pivot to the B2B site-diary variant.

## Biggest risk
