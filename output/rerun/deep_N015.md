## What the product is
A per-building annual pack for a Swedish BRF (housing co-op): the right filters and small vent parts for each apartment, plus a size schedule and maintenance-calendar entry, sold to the board or the property manager.

## Jobs to be done
- "Pass the OVK inspection without remarks and without us having to learn ventilation."
- "Put ventilation upkeep in the maintenance plan and know the yearly cost."
- "Stop residents calling about stale air / noise without a service call-out."
- "Show new members and the bank that underhåll is under control."

## Key structural problem: buyer is not user
In a BRF the association typically owns shared ducts, while filters in each apartment's supply-air vent are often the resident's job, depending on the bylaws ([Familjens Jurist on ventilation responsibility](https://www.familjensjurist.se/fraga-juristen/bostad--fastighet/ansvar-for-ventilation-i-bostadsratt/)). So the BRF only buys the pack if the bylaws or board policy put filter changes on the association, or the board decides to centralise it. Must be tested in interviews.

## Channel and CAC
- Channel: warm intros via ex-VELUX housing-association/BRF contacts, then property managers; no paid media.
- Cash CAC (ASSUMPTION): EUR 50-150 per signed BRF (samples, travel, a site survey). Founder time (ASSUMPTION): 10-15 hours per BRF to first order, which at a EUR 50/hour notional rate is EUR 500-750. ## Regulatory checklist (only what applies)
- OVK (mandatory ventilation inspection): 3-year interval for multi-family with FT/FTX systems, 6 years for F/FX/S per [Malmö](https://malmo.se/For-foretag-och-verksamheter/For-fastighetsagare/Obligatorisk-ventilationskontroll-OVK.html) and [Kiwa](https://www.kiwa.com/se/sv/tjanster/besiktning/ovk-obligatorisk-ventilationskontroll/); Kiwa's knowledge-bank page conflicts (no recurring OVK for F in multi-family), so verify against [BFS 2011:16 consolidated](https://www.boverket.se/Resources/constitutiontextstore/OVK/xml/Konsoliderad_OVK_BFS_2011-16.pdf) and the 2025 amendment [BFS 2025:6](https://rinfo.boverket.se/BFS2011-16/pdf/BFS2025-6.pdf) (not read). The pack supports, but does not replace, OVK.
- GPSR (EU product safety) for consumer-type goods; manufacturer/importer identification and traceability.
- Filter-class claims only if tested to EN ISO 16890 (from domain knowledge, not re-verified here); otherwise do not state a class.
- No health or air-quality medical claims (allergy relief etc.).
- Third-party brand names (Fresh, Flexit) only as "compatible with" (nominative use). Do not imply OEM approval.
- BRF procurement may ask for material fire-class and hygiene documentation (ASSUMPTION).
- Not applicable: batteries, CE electrical, REACH SVHC unless coatings or adhesives are added.

## The moat: what it really is
- Stated: founder BRF relationships + per-building size schedule in the maintenance plan + shared test report.
- How obtained: relationships exist already (claimed, not validated); schedule requires a site survey per building (about 2-3 hours each, ASSUMPTION); test report to EN ISO 16890 would cost an unquoted amount (not researched; do not commit before getting a lab quote).
- Cost / time: cash EUR 1.8-2.8k pilot (from the earlier pass); 6-12 months to 5-10 BRFs.
- How it is circumvented: (1) a local ventilation-service firm already visits these buildings and can add filters to its contract; (2) the schedule is just filter dimensions that a competitor can read off the old filter; (3) standard-size filters are sold at SEK 80 per 3-pack at retail; (4) a test report anyone can commission is not exclusive. A copyist needs the same access, not the product, and a service firm has it already.
- Honest rating: moat = switching cost created by service convenience, roughly defensibility 2.

## Biggest risk
Buyer/payer mismatch: BRFs may not own the filter task, and the property manager or a ventilation service firm already does it for less visible cost.

## Cheapest validation (about EUR 300-500, 4-6 weeks)
1. 12-15 interviews with BRF board chairs and 3-4 property managers via founder contacts. Ask who changes filters, what they pay today, and who decides.
2. Offer a free one-building size survey plus a mocked-up maintenance-calendar entry; buy the physical filters at retail for the pilot (no tooling, no inventory).
3. Pass if: at least 3 boards or 1 property manager commit in writing (letter of intent) to a paid pilot at EUR 20+ per apartment for spring 2027. Kill if boards say "residents do it" or "our service firm does it".
