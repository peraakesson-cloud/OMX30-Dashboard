## 1. One line
A numbered, limited-edition wooden jigsaw (about 300-500 pieces, laser-cut, irregular "whimsy" pieces optional) made from one living Nordic illustrator's artwork under an exclusive 3-year puzzle-category licence, sold at about EUR 79 inc. VAT, DTC first in Sweden and Denmark.

## 2. Moat mechanism
**Contractual exclusive licence (artist, puzzle category, 3 years, EU/Nordic territory) plus numbered editions.**
- Protects: that artist's work in puzzles, and the "official" edition status. It does not stop other brands using other artists, nor generic "Nordic style" puzzles. The moat is only as strong as the artist's own audience.
- How to obtain: shortlist 10-15 illustrators with an audience but no existing puzzle deal (check Instagram, their agent, existing licences); approach via founders' negotiation strength; offer advance plus royalty, edition cap, approval rights; get a lawyer to check chain of title (agent, publishers or past licensees may hold rights to specific images), moral rights, and exclusivity carve-outs (merch, prints) so the artist does not reject it.
- Cost (ASSUMPTION): advance EUR 1-2.5k recoupable, royalty 6-10% of net revenue, legal review EUR 0.5-1k. Time: 3-8 weeks negotiation.
- How it is circumvented: a competitor signs the artist's next works after 3 years; signs a different artist; or the artist leaves for a bigger publisher (Ravensburger-type) once the edition proves demand. Registered design protection is weak on a flat artwork, so copying is blocked by copyright only, which is enforceable against close copies but not "inspired by".
- Honest verdict: real but narrow and short; the artist, not the founders, holds the leverage.

## 3. Market size (TAM / SAM / SOM)
- **TAM EU (ASSUMPTION, Low):** EU jigsaw puzzle retail about EUR 1.0-1.5bn (top-down guess from a global market in the low single-digit USD billions; Ravensburger is the category leader, figures from memory, not re-verified). Premium wooden is a small slice, perhaps 3-5%, so about EUR 40-70m.
- **SAM Nordics (bottom-up, ASSUMPTION):** SE + DK about 16.4M people, about 7.5M households (population from memory, High). Buyers of premium design/gift puzzles: 0.3-0.5% of households per year = 22-38k buyers; at EUR 79 gives EUR 1.8-3.0m/year for SE+DK. Add NO/FI later for roughly double.
- **SOM year 1 (ASSUMPTION):** 10 founder-hours/week, one edition, mostly organic via the artist's following plus Shopify, 250-500 units = EUR 20-40k gross (inc. VAT), about EUR 16-32k net of VAT. Treat 300 units as base case.
- Confidence: Low.

## 4. Personas
1. **Design-minded gifter (30-50, urban SE/DK):** buys a EUR 60-100 object as a gift; cares about artist and packaging.
2. **Fan of the artist (25-45):** already buys prints and merch; the puzzle is a new product format.
3. **Puzzle hobbyist/collector (40-65):** buys premium wood puzzles for quality of cut and uniqueness; numbered edition appeals.
4. **Corporate/hospitality gifting buyer:** design hotels, interior shops, museum shops (small wholesale volumes).

## 7. Review-based gaps (could not fetch reviews; typical themes from knowledge, ASSUMPTION)
- Wooden puzzles: complaints about a burnt smell, weak or fragile pieces, loose fit, boxes dented in transit, no poster or reference image.
- Premium buyers want: heavier stock, consistent cut, reference poster, fair price, and no cross-border duties.
- Gap we could own: an artist whose images are not available in puzzle form, local Nordic stock with 2-4 day delivery, and numbered certificate. This is a style/provenance gap, not a technical one.

## 8. Channel and CAC (ASSUMPTION; no benchmarks fetched)
- Paid social CAC for EUR 79 gifts: EUR 25-45 (typical Meta/TikTok DTC range from knowledge, Low confidence).
- Organic via the artist's own followers: effectively EUR 0-8 per sale if the artist posts; this is the only channel where economics work. Ask for a contractual number of posts/newsletter mentions.
- Retail/B2B: design shops and museum shops at 50% wholesale (EUR 31.6 ex-VAT at EUR 79 retail) give a thin margin; use only as a halo, not as the core.
- Seasonality: Q4 gifting is 40-50% of sales (ASSUMPTION). Today is 11 Nov 2026, so a Christmas 2026 launch is not feasible (licence + production lead time); target Q4 2027, or a spring test.

## 9. EU regulatory checklist (only what applies)
- **GPSR (Regulation 2023/988):** EU responsible person, product traceability info, risk assessment, labelling.
- **EN 71 / Toy Safety Directive:** applies if marketed to children; position and label as 14+/adult to avoid it, but small parts warnings may still be wise.
- **REAL materials:** REACH for paints/glues/wood coatings; formaldehyde in plywood (E1/CARB-type claim from supplier test report); EUTR/EUDR timber due diligence for wood and plywood (check the EUDR application date, which was postponed; verify).
- **PPWR (packaging):** box and insert; recycling label as it phases in.
- **Licence chain-of-title and moral rights** (see section 2).
- **Consumer law:** 14-day withdrawal right; VAT OSS if selling across the EU; Swedish 25% / Danish 25% VAT.
- CE mark does not apply unless the toy route is taken.

## 10. Cheapest demand validation (under EUR 500 and 20 hours)
1. Pick the artist (week 1-2). Pitch a **non-binding letter of intent** (or a pre-launch option agreement for EUR 0-500) and agree to run a pre-sale.
2. Build a one-page Shopify/Carrd pre-order store with mock-ups (rendered, not photographed) of the numbered edition at EUR 79 with a refundable EUR 10 deposit or full pre-order with refund promise.
3. Ask the artist for 2 posts + 1 newsletter; add EUR 300 of Meta ads to 1,000 targeted Nordic users who follow similar artists.
4. **Pass criteria:** at least 60 paid pre-orders (or 150 deposits) at EUR 79 at blended acquisition cost below EUR 20 per order. Under 30 pre-orders = stop. Only then sign the licence and order stock.
5. Request a laser-cutter sample (EUR 150-300) to test piece quality before any production order.

## 12. Re-scored (skeptical)
Order: trend, defensibility, margin, repeat, eu, fit, channel_econ. Prior [3,3,4,3,4,3,3].
- **Trend 3:** puzzles normalised after the COVID peak; premium/gift/"slow hobby" is steady, not hot (ASSUMPTION).
- **Defensibility 3:** real contract, but artist-specific and only 3 years; no barrier to other artists.
- **Margin 3 (was 4):** about 38% contribution before CAC once the royalty, shipping and returns are included; not the 50%+ implied by EUR 79.
- **Repeat 3:** each puzzle is one-off, but a series of numbered editions and a second artist give some collector repeat; weak without proof.
- **EU 4:** simple GPSR/REACH compliance, EUR-wide demand, light regulation.
- **Fit 3:** pricing, negotiation, design, and sourcing are used; the building and robotics edges are not.
- **Channel economics 2 (was 3):** paid CAC consumes the entire contribution; works only if the artist's audience is the channel. Raises to 3-4 if the artist signs a promo commitment.

## 13. Verdict
**Conditional / test-first.** Worth running only the section 10 pre-sale, because the downside is capped at about EUR 500. Do not commit the EUR 8-10k until pre-sales prove at least 60 orders at low CAC. Biggest risk: the artist's audience does not convert to a EUR 79 puzzle and paid CAC makes every unit lose money.
