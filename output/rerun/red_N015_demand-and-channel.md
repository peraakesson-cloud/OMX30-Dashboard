# Red team N015 BRF Ventilation Year-Pack: demand and channel
Date 2026-11-11. Fresh research NOT possible: WebSearch budget exhausted; WebFetch hosts (familjensjurist.se, byggahus.se) not resolvable. Attacks below rest on the prior-phase evidence plus arithmetic and domain reasoning (labelled ASSUMPTION / Low confidence).

## Attacks
1. FATAL-ish: buyer/user mismatch. Prior phase itself notes in-apartment filter changes are often the resident's duty per bylaws (familjensjurist.se link, unread by me). If the BRF does not own the task, there is no budget line; the pack becomes a free giveaway. Many BRFs with FTX/F systems have no apartment filters at all (exhaust-only F systems have no filter in the apartment; filters concern supply vents/ FTX AHUs the property caretaker already services under a contract).
2. SOM math fails on time: 12 h per BRF x 5-12 BRFs = 60-144 h = 6-14 weeks of the whole 10 h/week budget before any recurring delivery; revenue EUR 5-12k at ~EUR 25/apt yields EUR 3-8k contribution, ~EUR 40-60/founder-hour at best, in year 1 realistically spring-summer 2027 (budgets set; annual meetings Apr-Jun).
3. Price anchor: retail 3-pack SEK 80-109 (~EUR 7-10) per Bygghemma/Hornbach (snippets, prior phase). Pack at EUR 22-28/apt is ~3x; a volunteer board treasurer will compare. Service value (size schedule) is a one-off survey, not annual, so year-2 repeat (~EUR 600) is overstated: once sizes are known, the board buys the cheap retail filter itself. Repeat is weak precisely because the "owned thing" is data delivered once.
4. Incumbent bundling: ventilation service/OVK firms and property managers (forvaltare) already hold the building access, schedule and invoicing; adding filters is a trivial line item. Founders' ex-VELUX relationships are with housing associations/roofs/windows, not ventilation maintenance buyers; the B2B channel claim is unproven and not verified.
5. Procurement: BRFs managed by large forvaltare (ASSUMPTION) buy via framework agreements; a two-person start-up with no insurance/references, no EN ISO 16890 report, faces vendor-onboarding barriers. Also Denmark (second launch market) ignored entirely; Danish andelsboligforeninger differ.
6. TAM/SAM unsupported: 25% buy-share and 25 EUR are pure ASSUMPTION; EUR 5-8m SAM is hence unsourced. Even the SCB apartment count (1.148m) counts dwellings, not ventilated-with-filters dwellings.
7. Compliance/consumer pitfalls: GPSR identification; EU packaging EPR (PPWR) and Swedish producer responsibility for packaging registration (FTI/Naturvardsverket) apply once you place packaged goods; filter-class claims need test (EN ISO 16890 quote not obtained); "compatible with Fresh/Flexit" trademark use risk. B2B reduces consumer-law returns but fit errors (wrong size) drive returns/credit notes across a building = whole-order failure, not one unit.
8. Seasonality: sales gated by annual meeting/budget cycle and OVK dates; lumpy, 1 selling window per year.
9. No moat above 2: access moat, copyable by reading sizes off old filters; test report buyable by anyone.

## Likely copiers
Existing ventilation service firms/OVK inspectors; property managers (e.g. large forvaltare) with private-label filter lines; Fresh/Flexit/Swegon OEM filter resellers; Bygghemma/Hornbach-type retailers bundling; Nordic filter e-commerce (unverified names).

## Revised scores
trend 2, defensibility 1, margin 3, repeat 2, eu 2, fit 3, channel_econ 2 (avg ~2.1)

## Verdict
Does not survive as a product business. Survives only as a free-to-run interview test (EUR 300-500): kill if boards say resident/service firm owns filters. Fix: become a data/software-lite service sold WITH a ventilation service firm (white-label partner) rather than selling filters; or find a proprietary-format consumable. Otherwise drop.
