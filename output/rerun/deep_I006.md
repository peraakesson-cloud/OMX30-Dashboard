## 1. Product and the real pain
Birch-plywood and clay block with replaceable paper nesting tubes; refills EUR 12-15; block EUR 29-39; landed cost EUR 6-9 (record). The pain, per the record's links, is that cheap bee hotels have tubes that cannot be cleaned or replaced and turn into parasite and mould traps. Whether a mainstream buyer feels that pain is unproven. Most buyers are gift or "feel-good" purchasers who never check the hotel again.

## 2. Market size (bottom-up, all ASSUMPTION, Low confidence)
Method: households x garden share x annual purchase rate x price.
- EU: about 200M households, about 45% with a private garden (about 90M). Assume 1.5% buy an insect or bee hotel per year (about 1.35M units) at an average EUR 20. **TAM EU is roughly EUR 25-40M/yr** for generic hotels. The refill add-on is not in this figure.
- Nordics (SE, DK, NO, FI): about 5.8M garden households. At 2% purchase rate that is about 116k units/yr, or **SAM of roughly EUR 2-3M/yr**. The premium segment (above EUR 29) is perhaps 10-15%, so **the serviceable premium pool is about EUR 0.3-0.5M/yr**. That is small.
- Climate caveat: the common red mason bee (Osmia bicornis) is realistic in southern Sweden and Denmark. Northern Sweden, Norway and Finland have a short, cool season, which cuts the usable SAM. Unverified.
- Seasonality: demand peaks February to May. Launching a test now (November) is well timed.
- **SOM year 1 for two part-timers: 250-600 units, about EUR 8-18k net revenue** (ex-VAT ~EUR 27 per block). This is sub-scale for a business and fine for a test.
- Trend: the Yahoo roundup says wildlife and pollinator gardening is mainstream in 2026 (link above, not re-read). No search-volume data. Trend score 3 is generous.

## 8. Regulatory checklist (only what applies; verify dates)
- **GPSR (Reg. 2023/988)**: applies since 13 Dec 2024. Need EU-based responsible person/manufacturer details, traceability, safety info on product/pack. Not a toy.
- **Timber**: EU Timber Regulation 995/2010 applies today; the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) covers wood products including plywood and its dates have been postponed repeatedly. **Check current application date and size category before importing.**
- **Formaldehyde / glue**: plywood glues. Check REACH Annex XVII entry 77 (formaldehyde in articles) applicability and prefer untreated or formaldehyde-free board. The product should carry no biocides or preservatives (also a bee-safety claim).
- **Packaging**: PPWR plus national producer registration for packaging in Sweden and Denmark if selling D2C from abroad. Check.
- **Marketing claims**: Unfair Commercial Practices Directive. Avoid "saves bees" or unsupported conservation claims; the entomologist endorsement must be written, specific and dated.
- **Consumer law**: 14-day withdrawal for distance sales; EU VAT OSS (SE 25%, DK 25%).
- **Do not sell cocoons or live bees** (animal and ecological rules vary). Sell the block and tubes only.
- Not applicable: CE, batteries, WEEE, medical claims.

## 9. The specific moat, honestly
- **Registered design (EUIPO)**: about EUR 350 for a single design (EUR 230 registration + EUR 120 publication, from memory; verify), about 1-3 weeks. Protects the look only. A copyist changes proportions, hole layout or material in days. **Circumvention: trivial.**
- **Tube format lock-in**: tubes are paper tubes of a standard bore (the science-based range is publicly described), so they cannot be patented (published prior art, no novelty), and generic tubes fit most blocks. A proprietary slot or cap format might make tubes block-specific, but customers can then be annoyed, and a copyist makes compatible tubes. **Lock-in is mild, as the record says.**
- **Entomologist endorsement**: a real credibility edge, cost perhaps EUR 500-2,000 for a review or licence (ASSUMPTION), 1-3 months. Not exclusive unless the contract says so; a competitor can hire another academic.
- **B2B relationships**: the only moat that is hard to copy, but it is the founders' network, not the product, and it is unvalidated.
- Net: defensibility **2**, as the audit said.

## 12. Differentiation we could own
Not a "better bee hotel" but a **maintenance system**: block plus a yearly tube-swap and cleaning schedule, backed by a named entomologist, sold to housing associations and municipalities as a maintained biodiversity measure. A facade or courtyard-integrated nest brick variant is a natural extension of the founders' building-industry experience.

## 13. Biggest risk
A copyist, or an existing retailer, launches compatible tubes at EUR 5 within one season, and consumers never verify the science claim, leaving us a EUR 34 item competing with EUR 15 blocks while CAC exceeds first-order contribution.
