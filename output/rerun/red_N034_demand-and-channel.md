# Red team N034 (estate-licensed reissue) - demand and channel angle

Evidence status: WebSearch budget exhausted, so no URLs were fetched. Every point is reasoning or memory, confidence Low unless stated. Nothing here is sourced.

## Strongest attacks
1. Fatal for the 10h/week budget: the product does not exist until an estate signs. 3-6 months of outreach to rights holders, with licence negotiation and legal review, is not credible at 10 combined hours a week. The 15-hour validation plan covers outreach only; negotiation, design adaptation, sampling, tooling and certificate production come after it.
2. The moat is licence-bound, not product-bound. The estate can relicense at term end, or to an incumbent who pays a higher advance. The founders build the demand and the licensor harvests it. Defensibility 3 overstates it; it is 2 until signed and 2 after, because it depends on a counterparty.
3. The target is adverse selection. Designers with unencumbered, original small objects are usually unencumbered because incumbents (Rosendahl, Stelton, Normann, Audo, Artek) passed on them or the commercial appeal is low. A "second-tier designer, weak name recognition" plan removes the very thing the EUR 79 price relies on (name plus story).
4. CAC is almost surely understated. A EUR 79 considered, gift-style purchase from an unknown brand selling an unknown designer: paid CAC EUR 25-40 is optimistic; EUR 40-70 is more plausible for cold traffic in SE/DK (ASSUMPTION). Contribution of EUR 24.7 per unit at 39% of net is negative after paid CAC. The "organic via estate newsletters" claim assumes estates have lists and will share them; most small estates have no list.
5. Repeat is near zero. One object, numbered limited edition. A buyer does not re-buy; the only repeat is gifting. Repeat score 2 is generous; it is 1.
6. SOM is fragile. 200-400 units in year 1 depends on a signed licence by ~month 5, leaving about 6 months, with Q4 gift season (the only seasonal peak for such objects) likely missed if signing slips past Nov-Dec. Today is 2026-11-11, so the 2026 peak is already lost and the next is Nov 2027, which pushes revenue to 12+ months out.
7. B2B channel claim is not credible from the stated founder edges. VELUX, Nilfisk and the building industry give no relationships with design shops, museum shops or estates. Design-shop wholesale at 50% margin leaves ~EUR 7 per unit and shops want established brands or exclusivity of a famous name.
8. Pitfalls: GPSR responsible person and traceability, the 2-year legal guarantee on a premium item, returns on EUR 79 gift items (gift returns after Christmas typically elevated; assume 8-15% for homeware online, ASSUMPTION, no source) versus 2 EUR per unit in the model. Packaging EPR and PPWR in SE/DK add registration cost per country, small but nonzero hours. Numbered-edition and "authentic" claims invite misleading-claims scrutiny if the estate chain has gaps. Originality of applied art (Cofemel C-683/17; Mio/konektra C-580/23 from memory, unverified) means copyright may not even exist for a utilitarian object, making enforcement weak against style-alikes.
9. Capital: advance EUR 2-3k plus 200 units upfront plus tooling is EUR 8.7-11.2k in the prior's own sum, at or over the EUR 10k first-order kill when contingency is added, for a product that sells maybe 200 units.
10. Market-size figures (TAM EUR 300-600M, SAM EUR 30-50M) are unsourced top-down guesses; competitor prices are memory. One-object volume of EUR 0.3-1M/yr is unfounded.

## Likely copiers
Incumbent producers who already run estate-licence programs (Rosendahl, Stelton, Audo, Normann Copenhagen, Hay-type brands, Svenskt Tenn, Artek/Iittala); estates that self-publish via Shopify once they see demand; vintage and Pamono resellers; style-alike sellers on Amazon/Etsy after a successful launch.

## Revised scores (1-5)
trend 2, defensibility 2, margin 3 (unit margin is fine, but only before CAC and returns), repeat 1, eu 4, fit 2, channel_econ 2.

## Verdict
Survives only if fixed. Do not allocate capital.

## What would fix it
A named estate with a signed or term-sheet exclusive in hand before any spend, covering a candle-holder or similar object with proven prior sales, a price of EUR 90+, pre-orders gathered before the order is placed, a wholesale or museum-shop anchor customer committed up front, and a licence that gives the founders a long term or renewal option, with no advance beyond EUR 1k.
