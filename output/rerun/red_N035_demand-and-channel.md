# Red team N035 - Exclusive-artist wooden jigsaw (demand and channel angle)
Date 2026-11-11. EVIDENCE LIMIT: WebSearch budget exhausted; WebFetch to wentworthpuzzles.com and libertypuzzles.com failed (host not resolvable in session). No URL was seen. Every number below is ASSUMPTION or arithmetic on the prior analysis; confidence Low.

## Attacks
1. FATAL-ish: moat is not a moat. Licence is exclusive to one artist only; any competitor signs another illustrator in weeks. Artist can also license cardboard/other puzzles unless the contract bars it. Fails the re-run bar for defensibility >2. (Conf Medium)
2. SERIOUS: paid CAC vs contribution. Prior own numbers: contribution ~EUR 24 vs CAC 25-45 => loss on every paid unit. Only works if artist audience converts; gate of <EUR 20 blended CAC is a hope, not evidence. Typical cold-traffic ecommerce conversion 1-2% (ASSUMPTION) implies CAC for a EUR 79 item well above EUR 30. (Low)
3. SERIOUS: artist-audience conversion. Illustrators' followers buy EUR 20-40 prints/merch; a EUR 79 puzzle is a 2-3x step up. Wanting a puzzle != paying 79. Typical follower-to-buyer 0.5-2% (ASSUMPTION): a 20k-follower artist yields ~100-400 buyers once, not repeat. SOM 300 units equals nearly all of that, one time. (Low)
4. SERIOUS: repeat is ~zero. A puzzle is a finished one-off; no consumable, no lock-in. Repeat only via new artist editions, each needing a new licence + MOQ. Prior repeat 3 is generous. 
5. SERIOUS: seasonality and timing. Q4 is the bulk of gift sales (prior: 40-50%); today is 2026-11-11, so the Christmas 2026 window is already lost (tooling/sample/licence 3-8 weeks + sea or air freight). First real selling window is Christmas 2027, ~13 months of tied-up cash/risk, or a weak spring.
6. SERIOUS: category trend. Puzzle sales spiked in 2020 and were widely reported to normalise afterwards (unverified here, could not search). Treating "puzzle" as a growth trend is a misread; the premium wooden niche is also served by Wentworth/Liberty (unverified). Prior "trend 3" lacks any source.
7. SERIOUS: B2B claim not credible. Founder networks are building industry (VELUX) and cleaning-tech (Nilfisk), not giftware/museum shops/hospitality retail. No relationship basis; wholesale at 50% margin leaves ~EUR 10-12 contribution (ASSUMPTION), which cannot pay for sales effort. Prior itself calls wholesale thin.
8. MODERATE: unit-economics optimism. EUR 8 pick-pack+shipping for a ~1 kg wooden box SE/DK cross-border is low; EUR 10-14 more realistic (ASSUMPTION). Wooden puzzles are fragile/heavy, higher damage and "pieces missing" claims. Opened-puzzle returns are effectively unsellable; 14-day withdrawal applies to distance sales (prior 1.9 return cost likely 2-3x too low for a gift item with Jan returns). Contribution could drop to ~EUR 15-18.
9. MODERATE: SOM vs founder hours. 10 h/week combined must cover licence negotiation, artist coordination, sampling, supplier management, content, support, fulfilment/3PL, GPSR file. 300 units over a short season is feasible in hours only if outsourced to a 3PL, which cuts margin further.
10. MODERATE: compliance load disproportionate to revenue. GPSR responsible person/importer duties if made in Asia; REACH/formaldehyde tests on plywood and finishes; EUDR timber due diligence (plywood is in scope; application dates were postponed, verify current dates for small operators); PPWR/EPR packaging registration in SE and DK (producer responsibility schemes, fees and registration per country, verify); import VAT/customs on a EUR 6.6k consignment. Each test/registration costs EUR hundreds, eating the 300-unit margin (300 x 24 = ~7.2k gross contribution before CAC, ~0 after).
11. MODERATE: Amazon EU. Wooden puzzle search is dominated by Ravensburger and cheap Chinese wooden/cardboard; unlicensed premium price EUR 79 has no organic discovery; Amazon Basics/private label not a kill here but referral 15% + FBA ruin margin.
12. MINOR: artist holds leverage; 3-year licence, advance EUR 1-2.5k recoupable gets lost if artist underdelivers promotion; no contract can force posts.

## Likely copiers
Any Nordic/EU illustrator-led brand or poster brand (e.g., poster/print companies adding a puzzle line); Wentworth/Liberty-style premium wooden makers signing artists; museum-shop publishers; generic Etsy laser-cut sellers with print-on-demand art. (names are from memory, unverified)

## Verdict
Only if fixed. Fails the moat test and the post-acquisition economics test as written. The only cheap thing worth doing is the capped pre-sale (<EUR 500); do not commit EUR 8-10k.

## What would fix it
- Contract moat: exclusivity across ALL puzzle formats for the artist, 5+ year term, first right to new works, plus artist co-marketing obligations (minimum posts, newsletter), with a larger-audience artist (100k+ followers) proven to sell EUR 60+ goods.
- A real B2B channel: signed LOI from 3+ museum/design/hospitality shops before ordering; otherwise drop B2B claim.
- Raise price anchor via edition/framed format (EUR 120+) or add a repeat mechanism (annual new-edition subscription).
- Pre-sale gate stricter: >=80 paid orders at <EUR 20 CAC before any order.
- Verify (not done here): EU puzzle market size and trend (Statista/Euromonitor/NPD), Wentworth/Liberty prices and EU shipping, SE/DK EPR registration costs, EUDR dates.
