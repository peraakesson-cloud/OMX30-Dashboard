## 2. Moat mechanism (the specific one) and honest verdict
| Element | How to obtain | Cost / time (all ASSUMPTION, Low) | How it is circumvented |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent BS 8423 test report (UK fireguard standard, "Fireguards for fires and heating appliances for domestic use", from memory, edition and exact scope unverified) | Send 1-2 samples to a UK/EU lab (BSI, Element, Intertek type labs; none contacted) | EUR 1.5-4k, 6-10 weeks | Any competitor can buy the same test. | EU registered design (fold profile) | EUIPO online filing, one design | About EUR 350 official fees (Medium recall) plus optional attorney; grant in days to weeks | Protects only the look of that profile. | Utility model | Denmark has one (brugsmodel); Sweden does not (Medium recall) | Only worthwhile if the folding mechanism is genuinely novel | Needs novelty; a plain screen is not patentable. |

**Skeptical conclusion:** the moat is trust-in-niche, not a barrier. A Danish or Polish sheet-metal shop can cut and bend a comparable guard with no tooling (laser cut plus press brake), and the founders' own tooling estimate (EUR ~1.2-1.9k) shows how low the barrier to entry is. Also, a UK standard has no legal status in the EU, and whether a stove-side guard is within BS 8423's scope is unverified. Defensibility honest score: **2**, not 3.

## 9. EU regulatory checklist (only what applies)
- **GPSR, Regulation (EU) 2023/988** (applicable since 13 Dec 2024, High): risk assessment, technical documentation, EU responsible person (founders are EU, fine), traceability marking, safety warnings, incident reporting. The guard is a general consumer product, not PPE.
- **New Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853:** to be applied to products placed on the market from 9 Dec 2026 (Medium-High recall). Easier claimant burden of proof; relevant to a child-injury product. Seek product liability insurance quotes before launch (cost unknown, ASSUMPTION EUR 500-2k/yr).
- **No harmonised EU standard** for stove guards that I can name (not verified). Fireguard references are national or UK; EN 1930 covers baby gates and is not clearly applicable. Do not claim "EN-certified" or "child-safe".
- **Chemicals:** REACH/coating (powder coat, no restricted substances), RoHS not applicable.
- **Labelling/language:** Swedish and Danish instructions and warnings; CE mark not applicable unless a directive applies (verify).
- **Heat/clearance:** guard must not create a combustible-clearance or airflow problem; installation instructions need to defer to the stove maker's manual. Surface-temperature test is the key engineering risk.
- **Marketing law:** no unqualified "safe", "prevents burns" claims; the Unfair Commercial Practices rules apply.
- **Medical claims:** none to be made (hard kill otherwise).
