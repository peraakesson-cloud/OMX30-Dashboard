## 1. One line
An exclusively licensed, numbered reissue of one small, previously unproduced or out-of-production object (candle holder, tray, desk object, wooden or brass piece) by a deceased Nordic mid-century designer, sold at about EUR 79 via Shopify, design shops and the estate's own channels in SE and DK first.

## 2. Moat mechanism (the specific one)
**Exclusive copyright/design licence from the rights holder (estate or heirs), enforceable under the Enforcement Directive 2004/48 (licensee standing, ASSUMPTION, check per country).** Honest assessment:
- **What it protects:** that single design, and the right to say "official, estate-authorised". It does not stop anyone selling "Nordic mid-century style" objects, and it does not protect a category.
- **How to obtain:** (1) shortlist designers who died 1957-1995 (copyright runs 70 years post mortem in the EU under Directive 2006/116/EC, from memory, High confidence on the rule; designers who died before 1956 are already public domain, so a licence adds nothing); (2) identify the rights holder via the Swedish/Danish visual-art collecting bodies (BUS in Sweden, Copydan Billedkunst in Denmark, existence from memory, verify that they cover applied art for the chosen designer), museum archives and the heirs; (3) clear the rights chain (who owns the *specific object's* copyright and any registered design or trade mark, and whether a previous licensee such as an existing manufacturer already holds exclusivity); (4) sign a 3-5 year exclusive licence for a defined object, territory (EU or Nordics) and channel.
- **Cost (ASSUMPTION):** advance EUR 1.5-3k, royalty 5-10% of net sales with an annual minimum, lawyer review EUR 0.5-1k. Optional EU registered design for new product variants about EUR 350 (EUIPO base fee, from memory, Low).
- **Time:** 3-6 months from first email to signature (ASSUMPTION). Estate responses at 10 h/week combined are the gating factor.
- **How it can be circumvented:** (a) the estate or the next heir licenses someone else when your term ends, or sells the rights to a large producer; (b) knock-offs of the same object in countries where it is public domain or the rights chain is murky; (c) a competitor copies the *style* with a different object; (d) the original may be covered by an existing licensee and your licence turns out to be non-exclusive; (e) CJEU case law (Cofemel C-683/17; Mio/konektra C-580/23 and C-795/23, from memory, not re-fetched) makes applied-art copyright depend on originality, so a simple utilitarian object may not be protected at all. Mitigation: lawyer opinion on originality of the chosen object before paying an advance.
- **Verdict on moat:** real, but it is a *single-object, time-limited* moat with a high chance of not existing at all (no unrepresented estate with an interesting object is identified). Defensibility 3 only conditional on a signed licence; 2 until then.

## 4. Personas
- **The design-literate gift buyer (SE/DK, 30-55, urban):** buys a EUR 60-120 object as a gift or for a shelf, cares about provenance and the "story of the designer".
- **The collector/enthusiast (35-65):** follows designer names, buys numbered or "first edition" items, joins mailing lists, pays for authenticity.
- **The design shop or museum-shop buyer (B2B):** wants an exclusive, story-led SKU at 50% margin, with estate co-marketing.
- **The estate/heir (licensor, not a customer but a gatekeeper):** wants respectful production, a named brand partner, minimum guarantee.

## 9. Regulatory checklist (only what applies)
- **Rights chain:** copyright term and ownership of the specific object (Directive 2006/116/EC), existing licences, registered designs and trade marks (EUIPO, DKPTO, PRV search). Written licence with territory, term, exclusivity, quality control and termination.
- **GPSR (Regulation (EU) 2023/988):** EU responsible person, traceability, safety information and warnings, in force since 13 Dec 2024 (from memory, High).
- **Consumer law:** 14-day withdrawal, 2-year legal guarantee, VAT OSS for cross-border B2C.
- **Materials:** REACH for metal finishes and brass (lead/nickel if skin contact); if the object is a candle holder, candle-fire warnings; if it resembles a toy (the Bojesen type), EN 71 may apply if marketed to children.
- **Timber:** if solid wood from outside the EU, check EUDR scope for wooden articles (application dates were postponed; verify current status, Low).
- **Packaging:** PPWR (Regulation (EU) 2025/40) obligations phase in from Aug 2026; check recyclability labelling for 2027-28 (Low).

## 12. Verdict
**Conditional / hold.** Do not spend capital until a named estate responds with indicative terms. The moat is real but its existence is unproven, and good-name estates are largely held by incumbents. Run the zero-cost licensor test first.

**Biggest risk:** no suitable, unencumbered object and rights holder is found in 6-8 weeks, or the estate's terms (advance, minimums) plus incumbent licensees push cost beyond EUR 10k.

**Cheapest validation test (EUR 0-300, ~15 hours over 4 weeks):**
1. Pick 15 candidate designers (died 1957-1995, second-tier names, small-object output) via museum and archive sites; check rights chain and existing licences (DKPTO/PRV/EUIPO searches are free).
2. Email/phone 10-15 rights holders with a one-page proposal; goal: 3 replies and 1 indicative term sheet (advance, royalty, minimum, term).
3. In parallel, build a pre-launch page with a render of the shortlisted object and a EUR 79 "reserve with email" button; EUR 200-300 in Instagram ads to SE/DK design-interest audiences. Pass: at least 60 emails collected at under EUR 5 each and 10 refundable pre-orders at EUR 79, plus a signed LOI from the estate.
Fail: no estate engages, or under 25 emails.
