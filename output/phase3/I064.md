# I064 - Nordic Bird-Safe Window Kit / Decal Sets (merged: I293)

Date: 2026-11-11. Confidence overall: **Low-Medium**. Web research was limited: Firecrawl had no credits, so only WebSearch snippets were available. No Amazon.de/.se pages or reviews were opened. Anything marked ASSUMPTION is mine.

## Verdict
**Weak conditional go - do NOT place the EUR 3k order before a EUR 300-500 demand/efficacy test.** The margin is excellent on paper, but the idea scores low on defensibility and trend, and the efficacy claim is the central risk. Phase-2 total was 3.29; my honest re-score is about 3.0.

## 2. Personas and jobs-to-be-done
1. **Villa owner with large glazing or a new extension.** Job: stop birds hitting the glass without ruining the view or the look. Triggered by a first collision.
2. **Bird-feeder hobbyist.** Job: feed birds safely. Feeders near glass raise risk; advice is to place them 4-5 m away ([Ökotest summary, via search](https://www.oekotest.de/freizeit-technik/was-bringen-weisse-streifen-auf-die-fensterscheibe_601675_1.html)). Gets the empathy purchase, but is price-sensitive.
3. **Apartment owner or housing co-op/BRF board (ex-VELUX angle).** Job: fix a glazed balcony or stairwell with a defensible, approved measure. Needs a landlord or board decision, so slower and more B2B.

## 4. Review gaps
No competitor reviews were read, so there is no review-based evidence. What I did find are expert-source themes:
- Silhouettes and UV markers are judged ineffective. The Vogelwarte-based sheet says UV markings cannot be recommended ([search summary](https://kommbio.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/merkblatt-vogelkollisionen-an-glas-vermeiden.pdf)).
- Sparse stickers fail. Spacing guidance: dots about 10 cm apart, 5 cm for horizontal lines; US/Canada guidance is gaps of 5 cm or less ([FLAP](https://flap.org/?p=7901)). Sellers disagree (Duncraft says 12-18 in).
- Expected 1-3 star themes: "birds still hit it", "peeled or faded", "static cling falls off outdoors" (ASSUMPTION).

## 6. EU regulatory checklist (only what applies)
- **GPSR (EU) 2023/988:** the label must show manufacturer name, address and email, plus a batch number. An EU-based economic operator is needed. Commission SME guidance: [C/2025/6233](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/6233/oj/eng) (secondary sources only; I did not read it).
- **REACH:** adhesive and film (phthalates in PVC, SVHC) - request a supplier declaration.
- **Packaging EPR:** SE and DK producer-responsibility registration for packaging. Check thresholds and national requirements. Small for an initial run; not verified.
- **PPWR:** light packaging obligations.
- **Claims:** UCPD/consumer law. Do not claim "bird-safe" or "prevents collisions" without a test. No medical, biocide or food-contact angle applies.
- **CE:** not applicable for a decal; adhesive-on-glass use is not a CE-marked category (ASSUMPTION).

## 7. Differentiation and copycat risk
What they could own: (a) a pre-spaced template that guarantees spacing of 10 cm or less, which is the common failure; (b) a daylight-preserving pattern (VELUX angle) that keeps the view; (c) Nordic species/design brand; (d) an independent test report, such as a Vienna WUA-style test, if affordable. The Swiss Vogelwarte and WUA have tested products ([BirdShades 2024](https://www.wien.gv.at/spezial/studien/wua/pruefberichtbirdshades2024.pdf)).
What stops a copycat in 6 months: **very little**. Printing dots is trivial. Only test certification, bird-NGO endorsements and SEO position hold up. Without them, defensibility is 1-2.

## Biggest risk
The efficacy claim. Expert bodies reject UV markers and silhouettes, and effectiveness depends on dense full-pane coverage. If a decal is sold as "bird-safe" without independent proof, there is both a legal-claims risk and a reputational risk when birds still hit the glass. Second risk: paid CAC exceeds contribution margin on a one-off purchase.

## What would change the verdict
1. A real Nordic demand signal: for example EUR 300 of ads or SEO to a waitlist page, targeting at least 3% sign-up.
2. A dense dot-grid pattern with independent test evidence.
3. A B2B or housing-co-op (BRF) channel that avoids paid CAC.
